This preliminary proposal makes sure your project is on solid ground before you build it.

A viable problemYou’ve identified a business problem worth answering

Early feedbackYou hear from me before investing significant time

Requirements

Proposals that miss these lose points before the content is read.

Format

  • A written proposal of 3 to 4 pages
  • Submitted as a PDF
  • Organized by the three sections below, using their headings
  • Name the file as for every submission, for example Lastname-Firstname-631-38-ProjectProposal.pdf

Data minimums

  • A sample size of at least 30 (n ≥ 30)
  • A clear target value or benchmark to compare against
  • Evidence that you can access the data

Evidence of data

  • A screenshot or table of the first 5 to 10 rows of your dataset, or
  • Summary statistics from initial exploration, or
  • Documentation from the data source confirming access
Using organizational data? Remove names, IDs, and anything confidential before it goes in the proposal. Summary statistics or de-identified rows are enough to show you have access.

Three sections, 25 points

Write your proposal in this order, with these headings. Select a segment to jump to that section.

Problem and data: 18 pointsMethodology: 7 points
1Problem identification and business contextWhat decision, who cares, and what you’ll test9 pts

1.1 Business problem statement 3 pts

  • A clear description of the problem
  • Why the problem matters
  • What decision needs to be made
ExampleRetailCo wants to determine if its customer satisfaction scores meet the company target of 7.5 out of 10. Management needs this information to decide whether to invest $500K in customer experience improvements.

1.2 Stakeholder analysis 3 pts

  • Who cares about this problem?
  • How will they benefit from the analysis?
  • What actions might they take based on the results?

1.3 Research question and hypotheses 3 pts

  • A specific research question
  • Null hypothesis (H₀) and alternative hypothesis (H₁)
  • The target value or benchmark you’re comparing against
ExampleResearch question: Is average customer satisfaction significantly different from the target of 7.5?
H₀: µ = 7.5 (satisfaction meets target)
H₁: µ ≠ 7.5 (satisfaction differs from target)
2Data source and availabilityWhere the data comes from and what it measures9 pts

2.1 Data source description 3 pts

  • Where the data will come from: company records, a public dataset, a survey, or another source
  • Whether the data is already available or you’ll collect it
  • The date range or time period covered
  • How you’ll access the data

2.2 Sample characteristics 3 pts

  • Expected or actual sample size; at least 30 is required
  • The population being sampled
  • Sampling method: random, stratified, convenience, or other
  • Any known limitations or biases

2.3 Variable definition 3 pts

  • The variable or variables you’ll analyze
  • How each is measured: units and scale
  • Data type: continuous, discrete, ordinal, or other
Evidence requiredInclude a screenshot or table showing the first 5 to 10 rows of your dataset, summary statistics from initial exploration, or documentation from the data source confirming access.
3Proposed statistical methodologyMethods, choices, and assumptions7 pts

3.1 Analysis plan 4 pts

Explain which statistical methods you’ll use and why:

  • Confidence interval: what confidence level, and why?
  • Hypothesis test: a one-sample t-test? Two-tailed or one-tailed, and why?
  • Significance level (α): 0.05, 0.01, or 0.10, and why?

Justify each choice based on:

  • The business question you’re answering
  • The type of data you have
  • The inferences you need to make

3.2 Assumptions check 3 pts

Discuss how you’ll verify each assumption:

  • Random sampling: how was the sample selected?
  • Independence: are the observations independent?
  • Normality: how will you check it? With n ≥ 30 it’s less critical.
  • Any challenges you anticipate with the assumptions

Tips for a strong proposal

The best proposals connect every statistical choice back to a decision someone has to make.

Start with the decisionName the decision and who makes it before you describe the data. A problem without a decision is hard to test.
Pick a real benchmarkYour hypotheses need a target value: a company goal, an industry standard, or a historical average.
Write hypotheses about µState H₀ and H₁ about the population mean, never the sample mean, and match the tail to the question.
Confirm access nowMake sure you can actually get the data and that n is at least 30. This is the most common reason projects stall.
Name your biasesEvery sample has limits. Saying how yours was drawn, and who might be missing, earns points.
Justify every choiceFor the confidence level, the tails, and α, explain why that choice fits this business question.
